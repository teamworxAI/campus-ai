# Bake-off B2, embedder half: EmbeddingGemma 2 against multilingual-e5-small on the full research graph (cards P7, R3; decision D7)

October 28, 2026, demo laptop (RTX 2050, 4 GB), `campus-ai:embed` image, models from local weights. Raw tables:
`backtest_20261028.md` (EmbeddingGemma 2) and `backtest_e5-small_20261028.md` (e5); probes `expert_search_20261028.md`,
`expert_search_e5-small_20261028.md`, `partner_search_20261028.md`, `partner_search_e5-small_20261028.md`.
The reranker half of B2 is in `b2_bakeoff_20261026.md` and `b2_bakeoff_weights_20261027.md`.

## Verdict

**EmbeddingGemma 2 stays the default embedder (D7 decided).** On the R6 partner back-test (100 projects, fusion only,
no reranker) it reaches recall@10 0.32 and MRR 0.133 against 0.29 and 0.126 for `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small`.
The plan's bar is that the baseline must clearly win to displace the default; it loses by three projects out of 100,
which is within the noise of this test (the two runs differ on 7 projects, 5 one way and 2 the other). e5 is also
text-only: it refused the 94 Europeana image nodes of the same records, so it could never be more than the text
baseline. Its only edges are speed (0.2 s against 0.3 s per partner query warm; about four times faster to index: 58 s against 234 s for the same 7,150-node file)
and 384-d vectors with no separate fast size, neither of which matters at this graph size.

| Embedder | Vectors | recall@10 | MRR | s per query (warm, GPU to itself) | First query incl. loading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EmbeddingGemma 2 (`google/embeddinggemma-2`, default) | 128-d fast (Matryoshka of 768) + 768-d full | **0.32** (32/100) | **0.133** | 0.3 | 22.5 s |
| multilingual-e5-small (`intfloat/multilingual-e5-small`, text baseline) | 384-d (fast = full) | 0.29 (29/100) | 0.126 | 0.2 | 14.4 s |

The EmbeddingGemma 2 figures equal the Oct 23 runs on the same graph (0.32 and 0.13 on 100 and on 200 projects,
`backtest_fusion_20261023.md`, `backtest_20261023.md`): the back-test is deterministic from one index. Pool
statistics (hidden partner in the fused top 50) are only printed by `--rerank` runs; this fusion-only run has none.
The B2 reranker reports put the EmbeddingGemma 2 pool hit rate at 76% of 50 projects.

## Setup

Two graphs with the same records, one per embedder, because Neo4j community has one database and the graph
store refuses a vector index of another size (`campus_ai/core/graph/store.py`, `ensure_schema`):

- **Live graph** (compose service `neo4j`, untouched): EmbeddingGemma 2, 71,942 nodes, 290,422 links.
- **Throwaway graph** `campus-ai-neo4j-e5` (`neo4j:2026.09.0-community`, the compose tag, on the `campus-ai_graph`
  network, removed with its volume after the run): e5-small, indexed from the same three files with
  `scripts/graph_search.py --backend e5-small index <path>`, 71,848 nodes, 290,219 links.

Records (from `D:/campus-ai/data`, the files the live graph was indexed from; their label counts sum to exactly the
live graph's 71,942 nodes): `openalex/sfu/records.jsonl` (7,150 nodes: 2,226 researchers, 3,000 publications, 1,906
topics, 17 SDGs, 1 institution), `cordis/horizon/records.jsonl` (64,416 nodes: 23,954 projects, 35,625 organisations,
3,808 call topics, 1,014 topics, 15 clusters), `europeana/salmon/items.jsonl` (376 nodes: 151 items, 94 images, 70
creators, 32 institutions, 29 collections). The 3,808 CallTopic nodes carry text in both graphs (the e5 graph took
them from the records file as it is on this branch; the live graph's call descriptions may have been loaded by the
`--label CallTopic` path of an open PR, so the two can differ in call text; call topics are not part of either
measure here). The two differences between the graphs are the 94 Europeana images with their 203 links, which
e5 cannot embed (`NotImplementedError: e5-small does not support 'image' inputs`), so the Europeana file was re-run
without its image records (282 text nodes, 420 links), and the possible call text above.

The back-test and the two probes take `--backend` since this run (`evals/research_assistant/backtest.py`,
`expert_search.py`, `partner_search.py`; the report file name carries the backend when it is not the default).
Container recipe: the `campus-ai:embed` image with the worktree bind-mounted at `/app`, `D:/models` at `/models`,
`NEO4J_URI=bolt://campus-ai-neo4j-e5:7687` for the e5 graph, `--gpus all`.

Timing caveat: the first EmbeddingGemma 2 back-test ran while the e5 index was still being built on the same GPU
(first query 712 s, warm mean 9.8 s); it was re-run on a quiet machine, with identical ranks, and the table shows the
quiet run. The e5 back-test and all four probes ran on a quiet machine.

## Per project

Of the 100 projects (the 100th differs between the runs on a start-date tie, DISCOVER-ME against BEACONSplus, both
misses), both embedders find the hidden partner in the top 10 for 27, EmbeddingGemma 2 alone for 5 (AdMar at 10,
BEST at 10, BlueOcean at 8, M-ERA.NET 4 at 9, PHANTOM at 10), e5 alone for 2 (ONLIVES at 5, THE MAKERS at 9), and
neither for 65. Each puts 15 hidden partners in the top 3. Among the 27 shared hits the rank moves by at most a few
places (EHRA 1 to 2, SAFE-MOVE 8 to 3, JOHN 2 to 5). So the two indexes agree on which projects are findable; the
limit is the task, as the reranker reports found (the partner is often in none of the fused candidates, or a top
project has 20 to 30 members), not the embedder.

## What the probes read like

Both embedders pass the probes' bar (a plausible expert or organisation for every question), with the same
questions and mostly the same names.

- **Expert search (OpenAlex, 6 questions, top 5):** 16 of the 30 names are shared. EmbeddingGemma 2 and e5 agree on the
  top expert for wildfire smoke (Ryan W. Allen), medical imaging (Weina Jin), superconductivity (S. R. Dunsiger) and
  on the housing and salmon sets. e5 adds Jonathan W. Moore (18 works on fish ecology) for salmon, which
  EmbeddingGemma 2 misses, and Kelley Lee (global public health) for wildfire smoke; EmbeddingGemma 2's Indigenous
  language list leans on place-based education (Skye Augustine, Michelle Pidgeon), e5's on Indigenous rights and
  decolonial thought. Neither has a language-revitalisation specialist, since the topics do not name one.
- **Partner search (CORDIS, 6 questions, top 5 organisations and projects):** 18 of 30 organisations and 19 of 30
  projects are shared; the project lists are near-identical
  (RESILFIRE, healthRiskADAPT, ATMOPOLIS for wildfire smoke; MIA-NORMAL, QUSTom for medical imaging; the same five
  superconducting-device projects in a different order; DIGICHer, REEVALUATE, WORTH for heritage). The organisation
  lists share the same weakness: for wildfire smoke both return national funding agencies whose fields say "public
  health", because fields, not projects, carry the organisation text. e5's organisation lists have more off-topic
  entries (a neuroimmunology company whose fields are "climatic changes; pandemics" for medical imaging; a Chinese
  university for superconducting devices), EmbeddingGemma 2's are a little tighter (Quantware, Supracon, Single
  Quantum-type device makers).
- **Speed:** both answer a probe in 0.1 to 0.5 s warm; the first question pays model loading (31 s and 22 s for
  EmbeddingGemma 2, 12 s for e5).
