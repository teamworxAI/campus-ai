# Bake-off B2: reranker and embedder (card R3)

October 26, 2026, on the demo laptop (Windows 11, Docker Desktop, NVIDIA RTX 2050 with 4 GB),
over the full research graph (71k nodes: 3000 SFU works with 2226 researchers, 24k Horizon
Europe projects, 36k organisations, 3808 call topics, all embedded with EmbeddingGemma 2).
Everything ran offline in the `embed` image; the only network step was fetching the reranker
weights once.

## Verdict

**The reranker stays off by default.** On the R6 partner back-test it does not lift recall@10
or MRR (0.30 to 0.26 and 0.150 to 0.131 on the same 50 projects), and it costs about 10 s per
partner query on this GPU against about 1.7 s without it. The bar for switching it on was
+0.05 recall@10 at under 2 s per query; it misses both. The api service's `ResearchSearch`
keeps `reranker=None`. The step stays in the code behind `--rerank` and
`ResearchSearch(reranker=research_reranker())`, because the probes read somewhat better with it
(expert search most of all) and the 4B model on the team's inference box is still untested.

## The reranker

- Model: `Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B` (default) and `Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-4B` (option for the
  inference box; it does not fit 4 GB next to the embedder). Both model cards declare
  `license: apache-2.0` and neither is gated (read Oct 23 on Hugging Face); listed in the D10
  register and `tests/test_licences.py`.
- Fetch: `scripts/fetch_models.py` now fetches it with the embedders; 1.2 GB
  (`model.safetensors` 1.19 GB), 82 s on the laptop's connection. It then loads with the network
  off (`--network none`).
- Scoring follows the model card: a causal LM asked "yes" or "no" for
  `<Instruct>/<Query>/<Document>` inside the card's system/user/assistant frame; the score is
  P(yes) from the last-token logits. The prompt's token ids were checked against the card's
  recipe (identical) and the card's example scores 0.9995 and 0.00001 (card: logit 7.6 and
  -11.4). Only the last token's logits are computed (the full vocabulary tensor did not fit
  4 GB). Long queries are cut to a third of the 1024-token budget, documents to the rest.
- Retriever: the top 50 fused (RRF) candidates are scored and ordered by reranker score, fusion
  score breaking ties. Partner search weights each matching project by its reranker score.
- Instruction for research search: "Given a research topic or project description, retrieve
  research projects, researcher profiles or funding call topics on the same research subject".

## Partner back-test (R6), same 50 projects

Most recent 50 Horizon Europe projects with a Canadian participant; the coordinator (or first
eligible partner) is hidden and must come back in the top 10 organisations.

| | recall@10 | MRR | s per query (warm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fusion only (`backtest_fusion_20261023.md`, first 50 of its 100) | **0.30** (15/50) | **0.150** | 1.7 (100-project run) |
| Fusion + Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B (`backtest_rerank_20261026.md`) | 0.26 (13/50) | 0.131 | 10.8 (first query, with model loading: 54.7) |

Over all 100 projects the fusion-only run gives recall@10 0.32 and MRR 0.13, as in
`backtest_20261023.md` (200 projects). The reranked run was kept to 50 projects because a
project took 8-10 s, over the 5 s limit set for growing it to 100.

Per project, the reranker moves ranks both ways: it finds 4 hidden partners fusion missed
(TRANSFLOW to rank 1, PHARM-N-ICP 6, SEC-EUR 10, AllergyREBELL 10) and improves 4 more
(IDPexec 7 to 3, SAFE-MOVE 8 to 4, SOLSTIS and ELVIS 2 to 1), but loses 6 (DECODE 1, TRACE 2,
KiTE 2.0 3, WATSAI 5, EDI-MSK 4, AdMar 10 all drop out) and slips 3 (BRAVA and ML4Plankton 1 to 2,
NANOBRIDGE 3 to 9). Net: two fewer found.

Reading the project lists behind three of them (top 5 matching projects before partner
aggregation, from a side-by-side probe on Oct 23):

- **BRAVA** (brain predictors of resilience to adversity in adolescence): fusion's list is
  already on topic (TRACE, NUCA, PRE-EMHPT); the reranker brings in DeMARRe ("risk and
  resilience in adolescent decision-making") and REATTACH ("resilient pathways in
  adolescence"), closer to the question. The hidden partner moved from 1 to 2.
- **GrowDeep2** (root mechanics in compacted soil): both start with Toughbranching and
  WATER-BLIND; the reranker then prefers FoodDrought and ROOTED over BreathingUnderground and
  DYNWALL. Similar quality; a miss either way.
- **SEC-EUR** (the 20th-century market for security devices, a history project): fusion
  returns present-day security projects (police procurement, LGBTI+ security, Israeli state
  crimes); the reranker returns historical ones (EU-GUNS on gun culture 1870-, SUSPICIO on
  colonial security politics, STEXEU), a clear improvement, and the hidden partner came in at 10.

So the reranker often picks closer projects, but the back-test asks for one specific
organisation, and the organisations in reranked projects are not more often the hidden one.
One reason may be the weighting: partner search sums the reranker's probabilities per
organisation, and these saturate near 0 or 1, so a few top projects dominate where fusion
scores were flat. Weighting by the reranked rank (as fusion does) is the obvious next try.

## Probes (read, not scored)

`expert_search_rerank_20261026.md` and `partner_search_rerank_20261026.md`, against the fusion
reports of Oct 23 (`expert_search_20261023.md`, `partner_search_20261023.md`).

- **Experts: better.** Salmon now brings salmon and fisheries ecologists (Price, Connors)
  instead of a geomorphologist and a law scholar; Indigenous language revitalization puts Mark
  Fettes (Indigenous and place-based education, linguistics) first; quantum materials returns
  superconductivity and condensed-matter people instead of diamond-material researchers; medical
  imaging returns more imaging specialists. Wildfire smoke keeps Allen and Delbari on top, then
  climate-and-health disaster researchers instead of a fire-ecology scholar.
- **Partners and projects: mixed to slightly better.** Medical-imaging organisations improve
  (Martinos Center, EIBIR, icometrix, Siemens Healthineers instead of a viticulture and
  ophthalmology firm); the housing projects are all on topic both ways; for salmon the reranker
  drops "Salmon Time" (Atlantic salmon history) for aquaculture and river-fish projects.

## Cost per query (warm model, GPU to itself)

| Search | Without reranker | With reranker |
|---|---|---|
| Expert probe (short query, 50 researchers) | 0.2-0.4 s | 2.1-2.5 s |
| Organisation probe | 0.2-0.3 s | 1.8-2.0 s |
| Project probe | 0.2 s | 4.5-4.7 s |
| Partner back-test (a whole project text as the query) | 1.7 s | 8-10 s (mean 10.8) |

Scoring 50 project texts alone (mean 292 tokens; query 336 tokens) takes 8.3 s at batch 4 or 8
with the embedder also on the GPU (1.4 GiB embedder + 1.1 GiB reranker, peak 2.9 GiB at batch 4,
3.3 GiB at batch 8). Batch 8 tipped the shared 4 GB card into system-memory paging (40-150 s
per query), so batch 4 is the default. Cutting prompts to 512 tokens brings 50 pairs to 5.2 s.

The first query of every run includes loading the models (50-55 s with the GPU free). The
first rerank runs on Oct 23 shared the GPU with another job and an earlier batch-8 back-test
that was paging; their figures (1188 s for the first expert query, 15-52 s per probe query) are
contention artefacts and were replaced by the Oct 26 reports above.

## Embedder half (EmbeddingGemma 2 vs multilingual-e5-small)

The full-graph comparison is **deferred**: it needs re-indexing all 71k nodes with e5-small
(a 384-d vector index, a second graph), which was out of scope for this run. The existing small
bake-off, run on the GPU in the `embed` image, offline:

```text
$ python scripts/embed_bakeoff.py evals/embed/sample_bakeoff.jsonl
6 items, 5 queries
embeddinggemma-2: recall@10 all=1.000, en=1.000, fr=1.000 (device cuda:0, torch.bfloat16)
e5-small: recall@10 all=1.000, en=1.000, fr=1.000 (device cuda:0, torch.bfloat16)
$ python scripts/embed_bakeoff.py evals/embed/sample_bakeoff.jsonl -k 1
embeddinggemma-2: recall@1 all=1.000, en=1.000, fr=1.000 (device cuda:0, torch.bfloat16)
e5-small: recall@1 all=1.000, en=1.000, fr=1.000 (device cuda:0, torch.bfloat16)
```

Six items and five queries cannot separate the two models; D7 stays as decided (EmbeddingGemma 2,
the only one of the two that embeds images, audio and video) until the full-graph comparison runs.
